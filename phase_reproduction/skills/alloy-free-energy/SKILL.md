# Atomistic free energies of substitutional alloys

Define the target as a coexistence point at specified temperature and pressure for a named potential and choice of nuclear statistics. Record the potential checksum, species mapping, parent lattice, allowed site defects, cell constraints, composition convention and energy zero. Record whether other crystal structures are in scope. A solvus restricted to the HCP manifold is a conditional equilibrium.

## Sample both chemistry and displacements

Let \(x=N_B/N\), \(\Delta\mu=\mu_B-\mu_A\) and \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\). Use \(B=\mathrm{Al}\) for Ti–Al. Canonical unlike-species pair exchanges sample order at fixed composition. Semi-grand single-site transmutations sample composition. Sample thermal displacements between chemical moves and relax the appropriate cell strains for the mechanical ensemble. For stress-free HCP allow the basal spacing and axial ratio to equilibrate independently. Distinct phases can have different cell metrics at the same pressure.

For coordinates with momenta integrated out and equal sampling masses, a symmetric single-site proposal has log acceptance ratio \(-\beta[\Delta U-\Delta\mu_{\rm cfg}\Delta N_B+\Delta W]\), where \(W\) is the full window bias in energy units. With unconstrained classical momenta, \(\Delta\mu_{\rm cfg}=\Delta\mu_{\rm phys}+\tfrac32k_BT\ln(m_B/m_A)\). This shift is affine in composition in the free energy and common to all phases. Document which field is reported. If species or move directions are selected asymmetrically, include the reverse-to-forward proposal probability. A trial move on a many-body potential must include changes to all affected atomic energies. Verify incremental energies against full energy differences for representative swaps, flips and periodic images.

## Anchor the free energies

Choose a route based on what can be sampled reversibly.

* **One connected set of windows.** Bias composition with overlapping windows and add a chemical order variable if replicas remain trapped at fixed composition. Reconstruct the joint, unbiased weights of composition and order (e.g. WHAM or MBAR). This fixes the relative normalization of the two free-energy basins directly. At a trial field, reweight by \(\exp[\beta(\Delta\mu_{\rm new}-\Delta\mu_{\rm old})N_B]\) and compare integrated basin weights. State the finite-size convention for symmetry-equivalent ordered domains and test the size dependence. An apparent overlap in composition alone can hide disconnected ordering states. A mixed-phase slab can accumulate coherency strain energy proportional to its volume. Use independent, relaxed homogeneous phase endpoints for the stress-free tie line. [Atomistic variance-constrained sampling](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.85.184203) and [integration across coexistence](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.86.134204).

* **Pure-to-alloy alchemical integration.** At fixed \(x\), make every atom interact as \(A\) in \(U_0\) and use the binary interactions in \(U_1\). Interpolate total endpoint energies at the same positions and cell, \(U_\lambda=(1-\lambda)U_0+\lambda U_1\). Use forces and stresses of this same Hamiltonian and accept exchanges against the current \(U_\lambda\). At fixed \(T,p\), the contribution to \(G_1-G_0\) is \(\int_0^1\langle U_1-U_0\rangle_{\lambda,T,p,x}\,d\lambda\). If \(G_0\) is the pure crystal with mass \(m_A\) and all site occupations are allowed, the labelled reference adds \(-k_BT\ln {N\choose N_B}\) and, with unconstrained physical momenta, \(-\tfrac32N_B k_BT\ln(m_B/m_A)\). Count these terms once. For a restricted reference, compute its actual occupation partition sum, including any sublattice field, and account explicitly for the release of restraints. Record ordered and disordered starts along the path. A jump in order with persistent hysteresis needs a reversible detour or overlapping biased windows. [Menon et al., Au–Cu, preprint v4, sections 4.2–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14795v4).

* **Absolute solid references.** At a chosen composition and cell, an Einstein crystal supplies an analytic origin for the positional free energy. Fix the translational zero mode and apply the matching release, permutation and finite-size terms for the chosen convention. A calculation with permanently frozen occupations gives the vibrational free energy of that decoration. Obtain chemical entropy by sampling exchanges along a path with a known reference occupation sum. An independent-site field can select an ordered variant at the reference endpoint. Combine the positional and chemical terms using the same constraints in both phases. At fixed cell this procedure gives \(F\). Convert to the appropriate \(G\) by cell integration, or by minimizing \(F+pV\) over the allowed strains in the bulk limit. [Frenkel and Ladd](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.448024) and [fixed-center-of-mass corrections](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9909162).

### Folded order windows for a D0₁₉ solvus

For four equal sublattices let k be total solute and nⱼ the sublattice counts. The order coordinate Q = 4(4 maxⱼ nⱼ − k)/(3N) folds the four translational variants into one basin. A harmonic umbrella on Q allows composition to fluctuate. At one temperature, cell-size and mechanical ensemble, samples with identical (k,Q) have identical relative weights across these windows. Retain per-frame volume, axial ratio and sublattice populations for equilibration and phase identification.

Locate a separator in the valley between the two peaks where both are visible, then keep this separator fixed while finding the field with integrated odds P(ordered)/P(disordered) = 4. Check equal total weights and separator sensitivity as N grows. Searching simultaneously for a new deepest valley and for an exact fixed point of the odds can jump between discrete minima in small cells. Compare independent starts and the crossing of mean-composition isotherms across sizes. Independent runs with zero umbrella at the inferred field can check conditional endpoint compositions. Their deliberately chosen starting-phase proportions supply no information about coexistence weights. [Finite-size phase weights](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.47.14757) · [Crossing estimator](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.68.1738)

For nested displacement cages, a broader-cage trajectory yields the tight-cage weights by retaining frames that satisfy the tight cage. At a fixed field the change in relative basin free energy is −kBT log[P(tight|ordered)/P(tight|disordered)]. Check that all remaining domain constraints are identical or inactive and quantify overlap in each phase. The probability that every site meets the tight bound can fall rapidly with N, making independently sampled cages more informative.

## Small-cell check with explicit chemical enumeration

Use a commensurate fixed cell at one composition to test the free-energy machinery before a bulk solvus calculation. Enumerate all arrangements of \(N_B\) solutes or use symmetry representatives with multiplicities \(d_s\) satisfying \(\sum_s d_s={N\choose N_B}\). Relax each representative at this cell and project out three translations. For a positive projected Hessian \(K_s\), the reference energy is \(U_{0s}=E_s+\tfrac12u^TK_su\) with normalized displacement density \(p_{0s}\). Define the integration domain \(D_s\) explicitly, e.g. by requiring each atom to remain inside the Voronoi cell of its assigned site. Use the same translation and momentum conventions for every decoration.

Draw displacements from a known Gaussian mixture \(q\) that includes the reference and broader scales. At the same coordinates compute \(\delta U=U_{\rm atom}-U_{0s}\) and \(w_\lambda=\mathbf{1}_{D_s}(p_{0s}/q)\exp(-\beta\lambda\delta U)\). Use normalized \(w_\lambda\) to estimate \(\langle\delta U\rangle_\lambda\). Relative to the unrestricted harmonic free energy the correction is \(-k_BT\ln P_{0s}(D_s)+\int_0^1\langle\delta U\rangle_\lambda d\lambda\). The endpoint estimator \(-k_BT\ln\langle w_1\rangle_q\) checks the quadrature algebra. Both estimates share the same samples and can miss the same tails. Check independent random replicas, effective sample size at each \(\lambda\), largest weight, sensitivity to proposal widths and the probability of approaching a site boundary. If overlap is poor, directly sample intermediate Hamiltonians.

Combine restricted free energies as \(F=-k_BT\ln\sum_s d_s\exp(-\beta F_s)\), using total cell energies in the exponent. Summing a subset of decorations gives that subset's restricted free energy and probability. Logarithms of multiplicities here supply the chemical entropy. Compare frozen geometry, classical harmonic and classical anharmonic results at the same \(N,N_B,T,V\). A single composition and small cell measure finite-cell ordering preferences and cannot locate a bulk tie line. The Ti–Al implementation is in the project under phase_reproduction/alloy_ti/pilot.py.

## Compute the solvus

Before interpreting atomistic TI as equilibrium of a fitted potential, test the configurational integral for short-range extrapolation. Atoms staying on their own Voronoi sites can still approach each other closely. A large effective sample size calculated from near-Gaussian displacements can miss a lower-energy region. Probe a broader independent proposal or a targeted compression path. Re-evaluate extreme frames with the original engine, measure minimum-image pair distances, compare energies per atom after supercell replication, and check whether single frames dominate the partition sum. The lower radial fitting parameter in an MTP file is a scale for the basis and does not impose a physical repulsive wall.

If the full potential favors collapsed configurations, distinguish the original Hamiltonian from a metastable crystal defined by an explicit integration domain. For a confined benchmark apply the same domain to all compositions, both phases and the harmonic reference. Report dependence on the boundary and independently sampled free energies. Changing the short-range potential defines a new Hamiltonian and requires evidence for the repulsive core. In the TiAl project, phase_reproduction/alloy_ti contains a replay of an extreme frame, a minimum-distance scan and independent sampling of a confined HCP basin.

For each homogeneous phase \(i\), use \(\omega_i(\Delta\mu)=\min_x[g_i(x)-x\Delta\mu]\) with \(g=G/N\) and the same energy and mass convention in both phases. At coexistence \(\omega_1=\omega_2\) and each minimizer supplies one end of the tie line. Equivalently integrate \(d\omega_i=-\langle x\rangle_i\,d\Delta\mu\) from calibrated reference values. Two isolated semi-grand sweeps at the same field determine slopes only. Fix the relative additive constant through a shared reference, a reversible thermodynamic cycle or overlapping windows. Last states before spontaneous jumps locate kinetic limits. [Fe–Cr example with thermodynamic integration](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2011.02.032) and [Ti–Al example reporting jump points](https://doi.org/10.3390/met16020140).

After establishing one coexistence point, propagate it at fixed pressure with \(d(\beta\Delta\mu)/d\beta=(h_2-h_1)/(x_2-x_1)\), where \(h_i\) is physical enthalpy per atom. Use a temperature-independent microscopic Hamiltonian for this form. Keep the two branches independently equilibrated and verify a second point from free energies to detect drift. The slope becomes ill-conditioned as the composition gap closes. Semi-grand nested sampling provides an independent route when other structures may compete and the available cell and sampling budgets can support a search. [Gibbs–Duhem integration for metallic alloys](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.75.014204) and [nested sampling with an Ag–Pd potential](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00477-2).

## Decide what the comparison measures

* Track composition, chemical order, local crystal structure, volume and axial stresses. For D0₁₉, track the four sublattice Al occupancies and their symmetry-invariant contrast, plus a local order observable when domains can cancel the global signal. Diagnose inter-basin mixing from independent ordered and disordered starts, adjacent-window overlap and trajectories. Refine the integration grid where the integrand changes rapidly. Use block or replica uncertainties through the entire root or tangent fit, including the common anchor. Test size effects at commensurate sizes. For \(\Delta x\ne0\), a per-atom phase free-energy error \(\delta(\Delta\omega)\) shifts the crossing field by about \(|\delta(\Delta\omega)/\Delta x|\).
* Compare the direct classical result with a CE constructed from the same potential using matched classical phonons, pressure or volume, allowed strains and occupations. For a stable harmonic mode, \(f_{\rm cl}=k_BT\ln(\beta\hbar\omega)\) and \(f_{\rm q}=k_BT\ln[2\sinh(\beta\hbar\omega/2)]\). Keep frequency weights and acoustic zero-mode treatment consistent. Refit or reevaluate the lattice Hamiltonian and repeat its phase equilibrium calculation when changing nuclear statistics. The quantum-CE to classical-atomistic difference mixes this change of statistics with CE truncation, treatment of vibrations and strain. A harmonic quantum correction to classical anharmonic free energies has its own approximation. Reserve a fully quantum benchmark for a converged quantum nuclear method.
* For a potential-to-DFT comparison, sample independent positions **and** occupations in each relevant phase and composition. Evaluate matched DFT single points and establish ensemble overlap before using free-energy perturbation. Statistical uncertainty in a converged potential-specific solvus, potential fitting error relative to the chosen electronic structure method, and model-to-experiment discrepancy address distinct quantities.
