For comparison, we evaluated the viscosity model of Schick et al.~\cite{Schick2012}.
Combining their expressions for the activation energy and the Arrhenius prefactor
gives, at a fixed temperature,
\begin{equation}
\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{Ti}}^{1-x}\eta_{\mathrm{Al}}^{x}
\exp\!\left[(1-x)\ln(1-x)+x\ln x-
\frac{\Delta H_{\mathrm{mix}}}{RT}\right],
\qquad x=x_{\mathrm{Al}},
\end{equation}
where $0\ln0$ is understood as zero and $\Delta H_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the
integral molar enthalpy of mixing, per mole of atoms. The pure-component
viscosities and the three enthalpy datasets are the same as in the KRP calculation.
The PBE and r$^2$SCAN enthalpies correspond to the original models used in
Fig.~\ref{fig:viscosity}(a) at 1873~K. The experimental enthalpy is the
provisional calorimetric digitization at 2000~K, transferred to 1873~K.
The pure-Al reference correlation is extrapolated above its validated range.
No parameters are fitted to the alloy-viscosity data.

Using the experimental enthalpy, the mean absolute relative deviation from
the five Takeda viscosity correlations at 1873~K is 13.5\%, compared with
43.2\% for KRP. The corresponding deviations with PBE and r$^2$SCAN
enthalpies are 24.4\% and 18.7\%, respectively.
The Schick model captures a broad intermediate-composition maximum, although
the measurements cover only $0.45\le x_{\mathrm{Al}}\le0.90$ and do not
constrain the Ti-rich branch. These residuals are descriptive comparisons
conditional on the pure-component inputs and the enthalpy interpolation.
